Incident #4471 was merged as one issue with one fix queued. Read one by one, the reports show three different problems. Each has its own cause and needs its own ticket.

Not one bug reported many times. Three bugs that look alike, because a date comes out wrong.

The three problems

  • parser (issue): the shared parser reads every date month first, and our tenants write the day first. A booking for 7 April comes out as 4 July. This is the large one, and the queued fix covers only this one.
  • hint (issue): the booking-v3 form prints a month-first hint under the date field. People at day-first tenants type what the hint says, and the form reads it day first. The date is swapped here too, but the fix is in the form, not in the parser.
  • clock (issue): the date is one day off, not swapped. These reports talk about one month only, an invite that came before the booking, one meeting that landed on two different days. That reads like time zones, not like day and month order.

Why one ticket hid them

The intake tag is a guess from the reporter's browser. In the table, every problem carries tags that name another problem. The desk also filed the one-day reports under parser by tenant and footnoted them. Merging by subject line (decision 0001) could not tell these apart: the subjects match, the causes do not.

Lessons

  • Count by cause, not by subject. The tenant and the form tell you the cause; the subject and the intake tag do not.
  • A footnote under a count is a second problem waiting for its own ticket.
